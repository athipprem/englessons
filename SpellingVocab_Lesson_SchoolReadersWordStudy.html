📖 FROM "LIFE IN SPACE"

Space words 🚀

These words all come from the glossary at the back of Life in Space. Read each meaning, then picture the word in a real sentence from the book.

astronomer
a scientist who studies the stars and planets
e.g. "Astronomers use telescopes to see distant stars."
gravity
an invisible force that pulls things towards the Earth
e.g. "Gravity pulls the spacecraft back towards the Earth."
orbit
the curved path a planet, moon, or spacecraft follows as it circles a star or planet
e.g. "The Moon takes about one month to orbit the Earth."
satellite
an object that circles a planet — this could be a spacecraft, or a natural object like a moon
e.g. "Astronauts launch new satellites and repair damaged ones."
docking
when two spacecraft carefully join together in space
e.g. "Once the modules are docked, the ports open."
friction
the force made when one thing rubs against another, which slows movement down
e.g. "There is no friction in space, so the spacecraft does not slow down."
booster rocket
a smaller rocket attached to a bigger one, giving extra power at take-off
e.g. "The boosters fall away once the rocket is going fast enough."
spacesuit
the special suit with a helmet that astronauts must wear to survive outside the spacecraft
e.g. "A spacesuit is like a mini spacecraft."
space station
a home and workplace built in space for astronauts to live and work in
e.g. "Astronauts live on board a space station, sometimes for months."
launch pad
the flat area a rocket lifts off from
e.g. "The rocket lifts off from the launch pad."
📖 FROM "HAVE YOUR SAY"

Debate & opinion words 💬

These words all come from the glossary at the back of Have Your Say. This book is all about sharing opinions and debating issues, so these words show up again and again.

argument
a discussion where people's opinions vary
e.g. "There are a range of arguments for and against homework."
opinions
what people personally believe about something
e.g. "It is important for people to share their opinions."
individuality
a person's own character, shown through the things they choose — like clothes or hobbies
e.g. "Children say jewellery is a way to express their individuality."
technology
inventions or tools that make our work and life easier
e.g. "Computers will help us prepare for high school and our careers, thanks to new technology."
socialise
to spend time and talk with other people (British spelling — notice the "-ise" ending, not "-ize")
e.g. "How will children learn to socialise if they use a computer all day?"
culture
the shared beliefs and values of a group of people
e.g. "Jewellery has been popular across all cultures and centuries."
interview
to ask someone questions to find out their ideas or beliefs
e.g. "Interview your family to find out about their homework habits."
games console
a machine used to play video games
e.g. "Many homes now have a games console as well as a television."

Spelling strategy: break it into chunks 🧩

Long words are much easier to spell once you break them into smaller chunks (syllables) and say each chunk out loud.

See a long, tricky word?
↓
Break it into chunks and say each one slowly. Which chunk is easy to get wrong?
↓
Circle that ONE tricky chunk in your mind and picture it separately. sat-EL-lite (double L), soc-i-AL-ise (-ise not -ize), doc-KING (single ck, not kk).
🔑 Three tricky spellings from these readers

satellite — has a DOUBLE "l" (sat-el-l-ite), not a double "t".
socialise — this book uses BRITISH spelling, so it ends in "-ise", not the American "-ize".
individuality — watch the middle: "individ-U-A-lity", two vowels sitting together.
