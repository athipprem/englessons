Why this matters 🌍

Reading is already your strongest skill — you scored 94% on your last school reading paper. This lesson is not about getting better at understanding what you read. It is about a very specific, fixable habit: turning a 2-mark answer into a full 3-mark answer, the way your school actually scores it.

Not a full sentence = 2 marks at most. That is your school's own rule, printed right on the test.

The three text types your school uses 📖

1. An information text with a table or diagram — facts laid out in short paragraphs plus a table you must read alongside the words. Your school's "Airport Runways" was this type.

2. A story — a short narrative with characters, feelings, and a turning point.

3. A persuasive campaign — trying to convince you of something, usually with headings, a statistic, and a call to action. Your school's "Brick by Brick" was this type.

The test format will not surprise you

Your real test will use texts you have never seen — but always one of these three shapes. Knowing the shape in advance means you already know what to look for.

Decision tree — is the answer stated, or built? 🌳

Read the question — find the KEY WORDS
↓
Find those key words in the text.
↓
Is the answer WRITTEN right there? → LITERAL — just report what it says.
Or must you PUT TWO CLUES TOGETHER? → INFERENCE — build the answer yourself.
↓
Either way — answer in a full sentence, using the question's own words.

Reading a table and the text together 📊

Route A (highlight): in your head, highlight the ROW the question is about, then the COLUMN. Where they cross is your answer.

Route B (three steps): (1) What is the question asking for — a name, a number, a day? (2) Which column holds that? (3) Which row matches the other detail in the question?

The 3-mark answer — Route A: see all three levels 🎨

Question: "Why did Mr Diaz move the Sprint Race to the Main Track?"

1 mark"The Main Track."
2 marks"He moved it to the Main Track because it is bigger."
3 marks"He moved it to the Main Track because it is bigger, and the text says the field was too wet after the rain."
See the pattern

1 mark = just the answer. 2 marks = answer + "because". 3 marks = answer + because + a real detail pulled from the text itself.

The 3-mark answer — Route B: A · B · T 🔤

Answer the question in a full sentence, using the question's own words
↓
Because — give the reason
↓
Text — name a detail or short quote from the text
↓
All three present = 3 marks.
"Not a full sentence = 2 marks at most." Your school's own instruction — say it to yourself before every written answer.

Drill R3 — story and campaign ✍️

The First Loaf
Mika had begged her grandmother for weeks to let her bake bread alone. The first time she tried, she forgot the yeast completely, and the loaf came out of the oven flat as a plate. Her cousins laughed at dinner, and Mika pushed her plate away, saying she would never bake again. That night, though, she crept back to the kitchen and read the recipe three more times, this time circling every ingredient with her finger before she started. The second loaf rose so high it nearly touched the oven shelf above it. When her grandmother tasted it the next morning, she smiled and said it was the best bread she'd had in years.
SAVE THE MILLBROOK POND
WHAT is it? Millbrook Pond has been a home for frogs, ducks and dragonflies for over 60 years. Litter and run-off are now choking the water.
WHO can help? Anyone! Families, schools, and local shops have all joined the clean-up days.
WHY join in? Last year, volunteers removed over 400kg of rubbish from the pond in a single Saturday.
HOW does it work? Turn up on the first Saturday of every month with gloves and a bag — everything else is provided.
