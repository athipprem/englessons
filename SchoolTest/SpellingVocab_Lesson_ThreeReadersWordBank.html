Three books, three question types 📚

📖 NATURAL RECORD BREAKERS — non-fiction, one natural world record per page (highest waterfall, biggest desert, longest river...). Full of science words like erode, dormant, plateau.

📖 THE DRAGON SKATEBOARD — a story about Tate, who is bad at skateboarding but turns a mistake into something brilliant. Full of action and feeling words like whispered, glared, stumbled.

📖 FREEDOM — a story about Austin, an injured eagle, and a hard choice between safety and freedom. Full of words like enclosure, desperately, injured.

The three question types your school test will use

1. Fix the spelling — a sentence prints a misspelled word underlined; you write it correctly.
2. Match the meaning — ten words, ten definitions lettered A–J; you match each word to its letter.
3. Fill from the box — a box of words above ten sentences; you pick the one word from the box that fits each gap.

We will practise all three, on all 45 words, before you meet the real test.

Spelling — Route A: beat blocks 🧱

Every long word is made of smaller beats (syllables). Say each beat out loud, and picture each one as its own block. Losing a beat is the single most common way words get misspelled — and it is exactly what has happened on your last two school papers:

🔑 Your own misses — the dropped beat

difference → you wrote diffrence (dropped the middle "e" beat: dif·fer·ence)
different → you wrote diffrent (dropped the middle "e" beat: dif·fer·ent)
uniforms → you wrote unifors (dropped the "m" beat: u·ni·forms)

Say every beat. Write every beat. If a word sounds long when you say it slowly, it needs that many pieces on paper too.

Spelling — Route B: Look · Say · Cover · Write · Check 📋

See a new word to learn
↓
LOOK — study every letter carefully.
↓
SAY — say it out loud, beat by beat.
↓
COVER — cover the word so you can't see it.
↓
WRITE — write it from memory.
↓
CHECK — uncover and compare, letter by letter. Wrong? Go back to LOOK.
🔑 Three traps to watch for in these readers

1. The dropped beat — your own pattern. Say every beat before you write.
2. British spelling — neighbourhood keeps its "-our" (not the American "-orhood"); this book's spelling is always British.
3. Sounds spelled by other letters — examined has an "x" that sounds like "gz"; imagination has a "g" that sounds like "j", and ends "-tion" which sounds like "shun".

Bridge: if beat blocks helped more, you can always go back to Route A — just say the beats to yourself before you Look-Say-Cover-Write-Check.

Drill R1 — type the word ✍️

Read the clue, type the word correctly. Get it wrong and you'll see the beats, then you must type it again correctly before moving on.

Meanings — two routes 🧠

Route A (picture the scene):

plateau
picture a mountain with its top sliced flat, like a table
porous rock
picture rock like a sponge, letting water soak straight through
dormant volcano vs extinct volcano
picture one volcano asleep, able to wake up (dormant) — and a different one gone forever, never able to erupt again (extinct)
an enclosure
picture a fenced area at a zoo, walls or fencing all the way round
an intruder
picture a bird swooping in where it is not wanted, uninvited

Route B (find the clue in the sentence): what else in the sentence tells you the meaning? A word never sits alone — the sentence around it almost always gives you a clue, an example, or an opposite.

Worked example

"The nomad's family never stayed in one place for more than a season, following the water and the grazing land." Clue: "never stayed in one place" tells you a nomad is someone who moves from place to place.

Drill R2 — what does it mean? ✍️

Pick the meaning. Get it wrong and a fresh one appears — every question ends on a correct answer.

Fill from the box — the routine 📦

A sentence with a gap, and a box of words above it
↓
Step 1 — read the WHOLE sentence first, gap and all.
↓
Step 2 — what KIND of word fits? A doing word, a describing word, a naming word?
↓
Step 3 — which ONE word from the box makes the sentence make sense?
↓
Tick it off in your head. Don't reuse a word you've already spent.
Warning: the box has extra words. Not every box word is used. Elimination alone won't save you — you must know what each word means.

Drill R3 — fill from the box ✍️

Three rounds, five gaps each, each with its own box. Every box has two extra words that don't get used.
