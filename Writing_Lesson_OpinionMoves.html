Why this matters 🎯

An opinion essay is how you win an argument on paper: in a school test, in a letter, even when you ask for something you want. A reader says yes when they can picture your reasons. Today you learn five moves that make an essay clear, and then you write one yourself.

A Longer Lunch Break
Tap to see the move 👆
OPINION

Should our school have a longer lunch break? I believe it should, because children need to move their bodies and they need a chance to make new friends.

Tap to see the move 👆
REASON 1 + EXAMPLE

Firstly, a longer break lets us move. After sitting at a desk all morning, legs feel stiff and minds start to drift. Last term, our class ran three laps of the field before maths, and Ms Lee said it was the calmest, busiest lesson of the week.

Tap to see the move 👆
REASON 2 + EXAMPLE

Secondly, a longer break helps children make friends. In twenty minutes, most of us only manage to eat. With forty minutes, there would be room for a game of chess, a chat on the benches or a football match with someone new. Arun joined our school in May, and he found his best friend at the lunchtime football club.

Tap to see the move 👆
OTHER SIDE

Some people say a longer break would leave less time for learning. However, a rested class learns faster, so every lesson we do have would be worth more.

Tap to see the move 👆
ENDING

In conclusion, a longer lunch break would let us move our bodies and make new friends. That is why our school should give it a try.

ROUTE B · THE FIVE MOVES

Those five moves, as steps.

Route A showed one essay. Route B is the same five moves as a list you can follow.

1. OPINION — say yes or no, and in a few words why.
2. REASON 1 + EXAMPLE — one reason, then something that really happened.
3. REASON 2 + EXAMPLE — a different reason, then something that really happened.
4. OTHER SIDE — what some people say, and your answer.
5. ENDING — your opinion again and your two reasons. Nothing new.

Which move is this sentence?

Did it really happen — a person, a day, a number?
Yes→EXAMPLE
No↓
Does it say what other people think?
Yes→OTHER SIDE
No↓
Is it what I believe, why, or a wrap-up?

What I believe → OPINION · why I believe it → REASON · wrapping up → ENDING.

Which move is it?

Pick the move that the sentence makes.

AN EXAMPLE YOU CAN PICTURE

An example you can picture

An example is something that really happened — a person, a place, a number. Something you could draw. If it says maybe… then… hopefully…, nobody can picture it.
AN ENDING WITH NOTHING NEW

An ending with nothing new

An ending only repeats what you argued. A new reason in the ending is a reason you never proved.
SAME WORD AGAIN

Same word again

In your rollercoaster story (Unit 49) you used “scary” 4 times and “fun” 4 times.

Same word three times? Swap one for a sharper word: scary → terrifying, nerve-racking · fun → thrilling, exciting · good → useful, helpful, fair · bad → harmful, unfair, unhealthy
