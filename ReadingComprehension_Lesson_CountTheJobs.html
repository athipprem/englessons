Why this matters 🌍

You got 12 out of 12 on the reading multiple choice at Unit 38. You understood every single text — that part is not the problem. The marks you lost were on the written answers. Not because your answer was wrong, but because you did one job when the question actually asked for three.

Look at your own answer:

What you wrote "From nervous to confident"
What earns all 3 marks "Niran started the show feeling nervous, worrying his voice would wobble. That changed when he stayed behind to figure out the cables and Mr Aditya showed him the sound desk. By the real show, he was running the lighting and sound with confidence, and nobody looked away when his name was mentioned."

Same idea, same story — but the second one does three jobs (start feeling + detail, what changed, end feeling + detail) instead of one.

And your "Describe two ways" answer:

What you wrote "Sign a petition or donate"
What earns all 4 marks "One way to help is to sign the petition at the library, which shows the council that lots of people support the skate park. Another way is to donate through the collection box at the community centre, which helps pay for building it."

Two names is 2 jobs. Two names plus what each one does is 4 jobs — and 4 jobs is what the question actually asked for.

You don't need to understand more. You need to do every job the question asks for.

Route A — The job boxes 📦

Elm Village School's library has stood almost empty for two years — the campaign to fill it again is called Page by Page. Question: "Why is the campaign called Page by Page? Explain your answer." (3 marks)

Every job the question asks for is an empty box. A box only turns green when the answer actually does that job. One filled box = one mark.

1 mark"Because of the pages."
2 marks"It is called Page by Page because every book donated helps fill the library one page at a time."
3 marks"It is called Page by Page because every book donated helps fill the library one small step at a time, the way a book is read one page at a time. The text says for every ten books donated, one stamped page is pasted onto the giant paper book in the hall."
See the three boxes

Box 1 = says what the name means. Box 2 = connects it to what the campaign is actually doing. Box 3 = a real detail pulled from the text. All three boxes green = all three marks.

Route B — COUNT · DO · TICK 🔢

Same idea as the job boxes — but as three steps you can actually use in the exam.

Question: "Describe two ways that you can help support the Page by Page campaign." (4 marks)

1
COUNT — circle every job word in the question: why · how · explain · describe · two · use details · change · learn. Here: "describe" + "two ways" = 4 jobs (name way 1, describe way 1, name way 2, describe way 2). Write the number: "4 jobs".
2
DO — one sentence for each job. Start with the question's own words: "One way to help is... Another way is..."
3
TICK — tick each job off as you write it. No detail from the text yet? Add one: "The text says..."
One job = one mark. Count first, write second.

Shape 1 — "Why is it called ___?" 🏷️

This shape always has 3 jobs:

Job 1What the name itself says — the picture in the words.
Job 2What that means for the campaign or story — connect it.
Job 3A detail from the text that proves it.

Worked example: "Why is the campaign called Page by Page?" → "Page by Page means the library gets rebuilt gradually, one small piece at a time (Job 1), the same way books are read one page at a time and the campaign fills the library book by book (Job 2). The text says every ten donated books earns one stamped page on the giant paper book in the hall (Job 3)."

Watch the trap that cost you Q47

Don't explain why the campaign is a good idea. Explain the name. "It's a great cause because the library needs books" answers a completely different question and earns 0.

Shape 2 — "Describe two ways…" ✌️

This shape always has 4 jobs:

Job 1Name way 1.
Job 2Describe way 1 — what you actually do, or how it helps.
Job 3Name way 2 — different from way 1.
Job 4Describe way 2.

Worked example: "One way to help is to bring a book from home and post it through the Book Box in the hall, which adds straight to the library's shelves (Jobs 1-2). Another way is to ask a family member to buy a book from the village bookshop, since it donates one new book for every five brought in (Jobs 3-4)."

The trap

"Bring a book or ask the bookshop" is 2 jobs, not 4 — two names joined with "or", with nothing described. That caps you at 2 marks out of 4.

Shapes 3 and 4 — stories 📖

The New Desk
When Tum started at his new school, he ate lunch alone every day, headphones on, eyes down at his tray. In week three, a teacher asked him to help sort the returned library books instead of sitting outside at break. Tum almost said no. By the end of that week, he knew exactly which shelf every subject belonged on, and two other pupils had started coming to ask him where to find things. By half term, Tum ran the whole returns desk himself, and he had started eating lunch at the table nearest the library, surrounded by the pupils who now knew his name.

Shape 3 — "How do feelings change? Use details." always has 3 jobs: start feeling + detail (lonely — ate alone, headphones on) · what changed it (asked to sort library books) · end feeling + detail (settled — ran the desk, sat with others who knew his name).

Shape 4 — "What does ___ learn? Explain using the story." always has 3 jobs: the lesson, in a full sentence (a task he didn't want can turn into something that connects him to people) · what happened that taught it (sorting the books led to being noticed and needed) · a story detail or quoted words (two pupils came to ask him where to find things).
