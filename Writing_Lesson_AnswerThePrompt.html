Why this matters 🎯

A prompt is a list of jobs. Every question in it is something the marker is looking for, in an exam, an email or a letter. Missing one is like leaving a question blank on a test. Today you learn a quick way to make sure every question gets an answer, and to swap spoken words for written ones.

ROUTE A · PICTURES

Every question in the prompt is a box.

Here is your prompt again. Each question has its own colour.

Think of your favourite place. What do you like about this place? What do you do there? How does it look, smell, and feel? Write a descriptive essay about this place. Use plenty of details.

Each question gets a box of the same colour. Empty boxes are things still missing:

LIKEsushi, Lego
DOthe rides, building Lego boats
LOOKtall Lego dragons, red and yellow bricks
SMELLwarm popcorn, sweet cake
FEELcool spray, tummy flipping on the ride

Notes, not sentences. Five boxes, five answers. Then you write, and every box has to show up in your writing.

ROUTE B · THREE STEPS

The boxes, as three steps

Route A showed the pictures. Route B is the three moves that make the boxes.

1. Find every question in the prompt.
2. Box each one — write a few words in its box.
3. Use every box in your writing — one box, at least one sentence.

Which way do you go?

Read the prompt→Is there a question you have not boxed?
Yes→Make a box. A few words.
No→Write. Then check: is every box in your writing?

Boxes are quick. Two minutes of boxes can save you several marks.

SENSES

See · hear · smell · feel

A good paragraph answers the senses the prompt asks for. Read this one first.

1The crate in our hallway was as long as my bed.
2Through a gap in the planks, I could see a row of yellow teeth and one cold green eye.
3A low hiss came from inside, like air escaping from a balloon.
4The whole hallway smelled of pond water and old fish.
5When I touched the lid, the wood was damp and slimy under my fingers.

Now find each sense. Tap the sentence that matches.
