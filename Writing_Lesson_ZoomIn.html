Your secret-garden story on Milestone 4 had a great twist — it was a prank! But the middle went past so fast that the reader never got to be there. Here are your own two lines:

“So he went to France and then when he arrived, he found another clue it was in Paris. So he went to Paris and then he found it in the Eiffel Tower the key.”

Look at how much happens: a trip, a second trip, a clue and a key — in two sentences. Nothing is stopped and looked at.

Stories that score high stop and zoom in on the big moment.
This matters well beyond one test: it is how every story you love is written. The writer slows down at the best bit so you can feel it. In this lesson you learn the trick, and in the next one you try it.
One more link
In reading, an action proves a feeling. In writing it is the same idea from the other side: you are the writer, so you choose the action.
ROUTE A · THE PICTURE

The camera 🎥

Imagine your story is a film. A writer chooses where to put the camera. Tap each picture.

Far-away camera = TELLING
“So he went to France and then he went to Paris.” The camera sees the whole journey, but you cannot see a single face, hand or sound.
Close-up camera = SHOWING
“Tom's fingers closed round the cold key. It would not stop shaking.” Now the reader is there, one moment, close up.
Telling is the far-away camera. Showing is the close-up.
A good story uses both: far-away to move quickly, then one close-up on the big moment.
ROUTE B · THE STEPS

The five checks ✅

On the last screen you saw the close-up camera. Now here is how to make one — five checks, one at a time. A close-up needs all five:

1
SEE — one thing you can see.
2
HEAR — one thing you can hear, smell or touch.
3
SAY — one thing someone says.
4
SHOW — a feeling shown by what a body does.
5
STAY — stay on one moment for at least five sentences.

The decision tree — use it on any part of your story:

Is this the big moment of my story?
No → tell it fast, in one short line
(far-away camera)
Yes → zoom in
↓
Run the five checks: SEE · HEAR · SAY · SHOW · STAY
