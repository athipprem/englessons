Why this lesson matters 🎯

Every story you will ever write — at school, in the ISA, or one you tell a friend at lunch — gets better when you know where the exciting part is and you stay right there. Today you get a tool for that, and you use it on your own story.

Your boat story had great ideas — a boat lost in the Bermuda Triangle and a mystery map! Here it is, exactly as you wrote it:

When some one found that boat they were surprised to see a boat so far from shore because it was in the Bermuda Triangle. Even though the surprise they knew and saw a map a and some kind of box. They investigated and saw a sick person on the boat so they quickly rushed him to the hospital then found out that he was dead.That map was also a way to go to Antarctica so they were very confused

Count what happens:

  1. They find a boat far from shore.
  2. They see a map and a box.
  3. They find a sick person.
  4. They rush him to hospital.
  5. He is dead.
  6. The map leads to Antarctica.
What this tells us
Six things in four sentences. The reader never gets to stand on that boat. Today you build a story the way high-scoring writers do: four parts, and one big moment you stay on.

ROUTE B · THE RULES

The four parts, as rules 📏

Same mountain, written as rules.

1
Beginning — 40+ words. Who is in the story, and where are they?
2
Build-up — 50+ words. What goes wrong?
3
The big moment — 80+ words and 5+ sentences, all on one moment.
4
Ending — 30+ words. Solve the problem with something already in your story.

On your story page, the computer counts the words for you. A part can't finish short.

Ending check 🌳

Before you write an ending, ask one question:

Does my ending use something that is already in my story?
↳ YES
Keep it. The reader thinks: “Of course!”
↳ NO
Go back. Plant that thing earlier, in the beginning or build-up — or choose a different ending.
DRILL 4

Which ending? 🎬

Each story has a problem and something planted earlier. Pick the ending that uses what is already in the story. Three questions; you can try again until each is right.
