Why this matters 🌍

Last unit proved you know the linking-verb rule — "tasted delicious," "sounded strange," even spotting it hidden inside a tricky "find the error" question. One verb still slipped: "smell." Today isn't new content — it's proving the rule travels to EVERY linking verb, in EVERY format, on the first try, not just the ones you've practised the most.

Worked examples — the two new verbs 🔍

look: "The old bridge looked (dangerous) after the flood." — dangerous, not dangerously.

seem: "The pilot seemed (calm) despite the storm outside." — calm, not calmly.

Error-scan drill — new verbs, same format 🔎

Check every sentence's linking verb before deciding which one has the error.

Which sentence contains an error?
A) The bread felt warm straight from the oven.
B) The choir sounded wonderful during the final song.
C) The children played quietly in the next room.
D) The old bridge looked dangerously after the flood.
Which sentence contains an error?
A) The soup tasted rich after the extra cream was added.
B) The room seemed emptily without the usual furniture.
C) The blanket felt soft against her skin.
D) The alarm sounded urgent throughout the building.

🎁 One more "Little" check

Remember: "Little" at the start of a sentence flips the subject and auxiliary verb around — "Little did he know," never "Little he knew."

A fast vocab reminder before the test: always check your answer points in the SAME direction as the clue, not the opposite direction.

"Even though everyone expected her to be gregarious like the rest of her chatty family, she barely spoke to anyone at the party." What does "gregarious" mean here?
