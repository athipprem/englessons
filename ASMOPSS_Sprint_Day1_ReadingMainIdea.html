Before We Start — quick check 🔍

One fast warm-up: can you already tell a "detail" from the "main idea"?

1. A story is about a girl who learns to bake bread from her grandmother, practicing every weekend for a year until she can finally bake it alone. Which is the MAIN IDEA (not just one detail)?
2. Which of these is a TRUE statement but too narrow to be the "main idea" of a whole newspaper article about a new city park?

Good instinct so far — now let's build a reliable method, not just a guess. Next →

Main idea: not too narrow, not too broad 🎯

ASMOPSS's favourite trap is putting a TRUE detail from the passage as a wrong answer choice, right next to the real main idea. Both "sound right" because both are true — the difference is SIZE: the main idea has to cover the WHOLE passage, not just one paragraph or one fact.

For each answer choice, ask:
↓
Is this true, but only about ONE paragraph or ONE small fact?
↓
→ TOO NARROW (a detail trap) — reject it
↓
Is this true of EVERY paragraph, start to finish?
↓
→ That's the real main idea
↓
Is this something the passage never actually said (sounds reasonable, but isn't in the text)?
↓
→ TOO BROAD / NOT STATED — reject it too
🔑 The 1-line test

Cover up the answer choices. Try to summarise the WHOLE passage yourself in one sentence before you look at the options. Whichever choice is closest to YOUR sentence is almost always the answer — this stops you from being pulled toward a true-but-narrow detail.

Worked example 👀

"The Library of Things" is a new kind of library opening in several cities. Instead of just books, people can borrow power drills, tents, sewing machines, and even karaoke machines — items they might only need once or twice a year. Members return the item within two weeks, the same way they'd return a library book. Organizers say the idea saves money and reduces the number of rarely-used items sitting in landfills.
What is the MAIN IDEA of this paragraph?

Options 1 and 3 are both TRUE — they're actually written in the passage — but each is only one small detail. Option 2 is the only one that covers everything the paragraph is about.

Author's purpose & reasoning ✍️

Some questions ask WHY the author included something, or WHY people in the passage did what they did. These aren't "find the fact" questions — they need you to connect two parts of the passage together.

🔑 Method: find the CAUSE, not just the EFFECT

When asked "why did X happen?" — go back and find the sentence that explains the reasoning, not just the sentence that states the result. The right answer usually restates the passage's own reasoning in different words; a wrong answer usually restates a nearby fact that sounds related but isn't the actual reason given.

A small seaside town used to rely on tourists visiting its beach every summer. But after a new highway bypassed the town completely, visitor numbers dropped sharply. Shop owners feared the worst. Then, a handful of them agreed to convert their empty storefronts into art studios and open them to visiting artists rent-free for a season. Within two years, the town had become known as an artists' colony, drawing a new kind of visitor all year round, not just in summer.
Why did visitor numbers first drop in the town?
Why does the passage mention that the new visitors come "all year round, not just in summer"?

Inference — reading between the lines 🕵️

Inference questions ask about something the passage never says directly — you connect two clues yourself to reach a conclusion the author expects you to reach, without stating it outright.

📝 STATED
Written directly in the text, word for word or in an obvious paraphrase. You could point to the exact sentence.
🧩 INFERRED
Not written directly — you combine two or more clues from different parts of the passage to reach a reasonable conclusion the author clearly intends, but never states in so many words.
Mrs. Alvarez checked her watch for the third time and glanced at the empty chair across the table. The waiter had already refilled her water glass twice. She finally opened her bag, took out a pen, and began writing on the back of the menu.
What can be INFERRED from this passage?

Full passage practice 📚

Floating Solar Farms

Solar panels are usually mounted on rooftops or spread across open fields, but a growing number of countries are now placing them somewhere less expected: on water. "Floating solar farms" consist of large rafts of solar panels anchored to the surface of reservoirs, lakes, and even old flooded quarries.

The idea solves two problems at once. Land near cities is expensive and often already used for housing or farming, so open fields for solar panels can be hard to find. Reservoirs, by contrast, are large, mostly unused stretches of water owned by a single authority, making them easier to secure permission for. At the same time, the panels shade the water beneath them, which slows evaporation — a real benefit in regions where reservoirs are a crucial drinking-water supply.

There are engineering challenges, of course. The floating structures must survive waves, strong winds, and years of exposure to water without corroding. Engineers have responded with specially coated frames and flexible anchor systems that let the rafts rise and fall with changing water levels rather than snapping under pressure.

Countries with limited land but large reservoir networks — including several in East Asia — have been the fastest to adopt the technology, with some floating farms now generating enough electricity to power tens of thousands of homes. Still, floating solar remains a small fraction of total solar power worldwide, and most experts see it as a useful addition alongside rooftop and field-based panels, not a full replacement for them.

1. (Main idea) What is this passage mainly about?
2. (Author's reasoning) Why are reservoirs easier to use for solar panels than land near cities?
3. (Inference) What can be inferred about most experts' view of floating solar?
4. (Vocabulary in context) In the passage, "corroding" most nearly means...
5. (Detail) According to the passage, what extra benefit do the panels provide besides generating electricity?

Day 1 done! 🎉

Main idea = true of the WHOLE passage, not one detail. Author's purpose = find the CAUSE the passage gives, not just a nearby fact. Inference = combine two clues to reach a conclusion the passage never states directly.
