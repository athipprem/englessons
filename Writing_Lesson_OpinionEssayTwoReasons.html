From four parts to five paragraphs 🧩

Your uniform essay only had ONE developed reason — and the "other side" paragraph was mistaken for a second reason. It isn't. Here is the real five-paragraph shape:

State it outright: a counter-argument is NOT a second reason. Reason 2 must be a completely new point FOR your position, not a response to the other side.

The planner — five steps, every time 🌳

START — read the question and find the two sides
↓
Step 1 — POSITION. Which side am I arguing? One sentence.
↓
Step 2 — REASONS. My two strongest reasons — one paragraph each.
↓
Step 3 — EVIDENCE. For each reason, a specific example — name a real thing.
↓
Step 4 — OTHER SIDE. What would someone who disagrees say, and why do I still think what I think?
↓
Step 5 — Plan first, then write. Don't start until all five boxes have something in them.
Model plan — "Should every school have a vegetable garden?"
POSITIONYes, every school should have one.
REASON 1Students learn where food really comes from, not just a shop shelf.
REASON 2Growing and picking vegetables gets students outside and active, away from screens.
OTHER SIDESome might say gardens cost time and money — but the vegetables can be used in the canteen, so the cost pays itself back.
CONCLUSIONFor these reasons, every school should make space for a garden.

Concrete evidence — two routes 🔎

Your uniform essay's evidence was a chain of guesses ("hopefully search it. Then maybe they'll join the school") — not a real example.

Route A — the picture test: can you actually SEE what you wrote? "Students concentrate better" — can't picture it. "A student who eats a proper lunch doesn't fall asleep during the 2pm maths lesson" — you CAN picture that.

Route B — name WHO / WHAT / WHERE, or a number: a real person, a real place, a real object, or an actual number, not a vague feeling.

Weak (hedged chain) → Strong (picturable)
❌ "Hopefully students would try the vegetables, and maybe they'd like healthy food more after that."
✅ "Last term, our class grew tomatoes in three weeks — students who had never eaten a fresh tomato were the first to try one from the plant."

The conclusion must match 🔗

Your uniform essay's conclusion introduced a brand-new reason ("so it is fair") that was never actually argued in the essay. A conclusion only restates reasons you already made.

Smuggled-in reason → Fixed
❌ "For these reasons, uniforms should stay, so it is fair." ("fair" was never argued anywhere above)
✅ "For these reasons — saving families money and helping students learn where food comes from — every school should have a vegetable garden." (only restates the two reasons actually argued)
Rule: before you write your conclusion, reread your two reason paragraphs. Your conclusion may only mention what's already there.

Written voice, not spoken voice 🗣️

Your uniform essay had "So like," slip in — that's how you'd talk to a friend, not how you write a formal essay. And a funny image was used as if it were real evidence, which weakens an argument instead of strengthening it.

Spoken → Written
❌ "So like, uniforms are kind of annoying I guess."
✅ "However, uniforms can feel restrictive for some students."

A funny image is entertaining, but it is not evidence. Save the jokes for a story — an opinion essay needs real examples, not laughs.

Openers, connectives — and the Instead/Moreover check 🔤

Your uniform essay repeated the same sentence-opener several times. Match each connective to its paragraph job:

Firstly — opens Reason 1
For example — introduces evidence
Some people believe — opens the Other Side
However — turns back to your own view
For these reasons — opens the Conclusion

One more habit — Instead vs Moreover. SWAP (one thing replaces another) → Instead. ADD (another matching point) → Moreover. Two quick checks:

"The garden was going to grow only tomatoes. ___, the class decided to add carrots and herbs as well." Which word?
"The class had planned to plant in spring. ___, a late frost meant they planted in early summer instead." Which word?

The proofread routine 🔍

Your uniform essay also had a conventions slip — a fragment starting "Because if..." with no main part. Four passes, every single essay:

Read your finished essay once, slowly
↓
Pass 1 — does EVERY paragraph have a topic sentence?
↓
Pass 2 — do two sentences in a row start the same way?
↓
Pass 3 — does my conclusion only mention reasons I actually argued?
↓
Pass 4 — read it in your head. Any sentence starting "Because" with no main part following it?
