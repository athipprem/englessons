Unit 30 · Lesson 2
Why this matters 🎯

Most error-spotting sentences you see are easy to clear once you know the trick. Today is about the handful that are genuinely tricky even once you know the tricks — small, easy-to-miss word choices that native speakers get right by instinct but that need a specific rule for anyone still learning them.

Before We Start — quick recap 🔍

A reminder of your strongest routine:

Which sentence has the error? A) "If the lights had been fixed sooner, the show would have started on time." B) "If the lights had been fixed sooner, the show would start on time."

Right — B mismatches "had been fixed" (3rd) with plain "would start" (2nd-type). This check is fully automatic for you now. Next →

New rule 1 — after "smelled/looked/tasted/felt," use a describing-word, not an action-word ✋

Some verbs (smell, look, taste, feel, seem, sound) don't describe an ACTION — they describe a STATE, like a describing-word does. After these verbs, use the plain describing-word form (adjective), not the "-ly" action-word form (adverb).

Does the verb describe an action being done, or a STATE/how something IS?
↓
STATE (smelled, looked, tasted, felt, seemed) → use the plain describing-word (unsteady, strange, sweet)
↓
ACTION (walked, spoke, moved) → the "-ly" word is fine (walked unsteadily)
Which fits? "After the long hike, her legs felt ___."

New rule 2 — "much" alone can't sit in front of "-ing" 🚫

The word "much" by itself sounds natural before a plain describing-word ("much bigger") but NOT directly in front of a verb+"-ing" phrase like "looking forward to" or "enjoying." In that spot, it needs a partner: "very much."

Which fits? "I was ___ looking forward to the school trip."
🔑 Quick test

If "much" sits right before an "-ing" verb phrase, it almost always needs "very" attached to it. Say the sentence in your head both ways — "much looking forward to" sounds incomplete, "very much looking forward to" sounds finished.

Spot the error — practice round 🕵️

Read carefully. One option in each item breaks one of today's two new rules.

Which sentence contains an error? A) "The soup tasted delicious after simmering all afternoon." B) "The soup tasted deliciously after simmering all afternoon." C) "The chef stirred the soup carefully before serving it." D) "The soup smelled wonderful the moment it started boiling."
Which sentence contains an error? A) "She was very much relieved when the results finally arrived." B) "He walked quickly to catch the last bus home." C) "They were much enjoying the fireworks display over the harbour." D) "The garden looked beautiful just after the rain."
Which sentence contains an error? A) "The old floorboards felt loose underfoot." B) "The bread smelled stale despite being bought that morning." C) "The dancers moved gracefully across the stage." D) "The music sounded strangely after the speaker crackled."

Vocabulary check-in — precise near-synonyms 🎯

A quick maintenance check — your vocabulary is one of your strongest skills, so this is just keeping it sharp on the trickiest kind of item: picking the near-synonym that matches EXACTLY, not just roughly.

"The old bridge was structurally unsound, putting every driver who crossed it in ___." Which word fits best?
"His explanation of the accident was so ___ that the investigators dismissed it immediately." Which word fits best (meaning "clearly false/misleading")?
