Route B · Find the clue in the sentence

You cannot always draw a picture. When you meet a new word, cover it and look at the words around it.

1
COVER the new word.
2
FIND the clue words next to it.
3
GUESS a word that fits, then CHECK it makes sense.
Snow blocked the only road, so the mountain hut was inaccessible until spring.
Clue: Snow blocked the only road → nobody can get there → inaccessible = impossible or very hard to reach.
The librarian was so absent-minded that she wore two different shoes.
Clue: wore two different shoes → she forgot to check → absent-minded = often forgetting things because you are thinking about something else.
The toy was so durable that it survived being dropped, kicked and sat on.
Clue: survived being dropped, kicked and sat on → it did not break → durable = strong enough to last a long time.

THE WORD BOX · A PLAN

How to fill from the box

1
READ the whole sentence first, to the full stop.
2
DECIDE what kind of word fits the gap (use the tree below).
3
TRY a box word. Read the sentence again. Does it make sense?
4
TICK it off the box.

The box has extra words. You can’t win the last gap by crossing out — every gap must make sense.

What kind of word fits?

A thing or an event? (comes after “a”, “the” or “this”) → a naming word. e.g. chaos, terrain, puncture, adjustment, champion
Something that is done? (comes after “can”, “will” or a name) → a doing word. e.g. muttered, ventured, absorb
Tells you about a thing? (comes before a naming word, or after “was”) → a describing word. e.g. rugged, durable, reflective, protective
Tells you how, or where? → a how word. e.g. warily, feverishly, frantically, overseas
Worked example. BOX: warily · adjustment · reflective
The mouse crept ______ past the sleeping cat.
Step 1: read it all. Step 2: “crept” is a doing word, so the gap says how the mouse crept → a how-word. Step 3: warily fits: the mouse crept carefully, because the cat could wake up. Step 4: tick off warily.

BEFORE THURSDAY

Before Thursday — your school reading paper

Thursday’s test has a reading part too. On your school’s last reading test, Assessment #3, you scored 49 out of 50. Here are two answers from it, and what the school said.

The questionExplain why Jack’s mother was an honest and just person.
Your answerJack’s mother is honest because she took all the stuff that Jack stole back to Gavin and then later apologised and they became friends.
Your markSchool: 2/2.
⭐ You proved “honest” with what she did.
✏️ The question asked for two things: honest and just. “Just” means fair. She was fair because she gave back what Jack took and made sure he was punished. When a question joins two words with “and”, answer both.
The questionWhy was Jack’s mother angry with Jack?
Your answerBecause, Jack kept stealing from Gavin and Jack’s mother had to clean up the mess.
Your markSchool: 2/2.
✏️ The story never says Jack kept stealing from Gavin. It says “another of your messes”. Say only what the text says.

Two habits for Thursday: answer both halves when a question says “and”, and say only what the text says.
