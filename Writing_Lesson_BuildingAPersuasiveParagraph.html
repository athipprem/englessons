The four-part structure 🏗️

A strong persuasive paragraph always has the same four parts, in this order:

1. OPINION — say clearly what you think, right at the start.
↓
2. REASON + EXAMPLE — give a reason, and back it up with a real example or detail.
↓
3. ANOTHER SIDE — briefly show you understand the other opinion too (this is what makes an argument strong, not weak).
↓
4. CONCLUSION — restate your opinion in a slightly different way, to finish strongly.
🔑 Why part 3 matters

It might feel strange to mention the OTHER side of the argument when you're trying to convince someone — but "Have Your Say" always shows both sides. Acknowledging the other opinion (then explaining why you still think what you think) makes your writing sound thoughtful, not one-sided.

A model paragraph, labelled 📋

Here is a persuasive paragraph about school canteens, with each part labelled:

OPINIONI believe school canteens should offer more fruit and vegetable choices at lunchtime.

REASON + EXAMPLEFirstly, eating healthy food helps students concentrate better in class. For example, a student who eats a balanced lunch with fruit is less likely to feel tired during afternoon lessons than a student who only eats chips.

ANOTHER SIDESome students might say that healthy food is less exciting than foods like pizza or chips. However, canteens could still offer these foods sometimes, while making fruit and vegetables easier to choose every day.

CONCLUSIONFor these reasons, I think school canteens should make healthy food the easiest option, without banning treats completely.
🔑 Notice

The connective words "Firstly," "For example," "Some students might say," and "However" all signal exactly which part of the structure you're in. Using connectives like these makes your paragraph easy to follow.
