Why this matters 📋

Every real ASMOPSS paper includes 2 short "real-world" texts before the long passage — a notice, an advertisement, or a handwritten note. They look easy because they're short, but they hide precise logic traps: WHO does WHAT, for WHOM, and BY WHEN. On the 2025 paper, the note-based question (who owes whom money) was one you got right — today makes that reliable, not lucky, and adds a quick refresher on connector logic (but/so), which cost you one mark on that same paper's Grammar section.

Reading real-world texts: the 4-question method 🔎

Notices, ads, and notes are short — but every word is doing a job. Before answering, quickly answer 4 questions about the text yourself:

Read the whole short text once, then ask:
↓
WHO wrote this, and WHO is it for?
↓
WHAT exactly is being asked/offered/announced?
↓
BY WHEN, or under what CONDITION?
↓
Is there a promise, exchange, or "who-owes-whom" detail?
↓
Now check each answer choice against these 4 facts, one at a time
🔑 The trap to watch for

Wrong answers often FLIP a detail — swapping who does the action, or reversing a condition (e.g. "before Friday" becomes "after Friday" in a wrong choice). Read every option against the text itself, not against your memory of the text.

Practice — a club sign-up notice 📌

CHESS CLUB — NEW MEMBERS WANTED

Sign-up sheets are on the library noticeboard. Return your completed sheet to Mr. Reyes by Wednesday to guarantee a place at the first meeting. Sheets returned after Wednesday will be placed on a waiting list, and a place will only be offered if space remains.
1. Which statement is correct?
2. Who should the completed sign-up sheet be given to?

Practice — a handwritten note ✉️

Real-world notes between family members are a favourite ASMOPSS format — short, casual, but with a precise detail buried inside.

Dad,

Could you charge my tablet when you plug in your laptop tonight? I left the charger on my desk.
I'll do the dishes tomorrow to make up for it!

— Mika
What will Mika's dad most likely do?
What will Mika do tomorrow, according to the note?

Quick refresher — connector logic in Grammar error-ID ⚡

One ASMOPSS error-ID trap swaps a RESULT connector ("so," "therefore") for a CONTRAST connector ("but," "however") — or the reverse. You already know this family well from Cloze; today's twist is spotting it inside a labelled A/B/C/D error-ID sentence instead of a fill-in-the-blank.

🔑 The 1-second check

Read the idea BEFORE the connector and the idea AFTER it. Do they agree/continue the same direction (→ needs RESULT: so, therefore) or do they clash/surprise (→ needs CONTRAST: but, however, yet)? If the connector doesn't match that relationship, it's the error.

Which underlined part is grammatically/logically wrong? "Many (A) local dishes are (B) surprisingly spicy, (C) so they can (D) taste quite mild to visitors who are used to hot food."
Which underlined part is grammatically/logically wrong? "The bridge was (A) closed for repairs all week, (B) however traffic (C) was diverted through the old town centre (D) for several days."

Day 2 done! 🎉

Real-world texts: pin down WHO, WHAT, BY WHEN, and any who-owes-whom detail before checking answer choices — watch for choices that FLIP a detail. Connector logic: check whether the two ideas agree (needs a RESULT word) or clash (needs a CONTRAST word).
