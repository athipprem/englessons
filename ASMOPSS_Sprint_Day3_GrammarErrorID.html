Why this matters ⚙️

On the real 2024 paper, Grammar crashed to 2/6 — but every single miss was a mechanic never taught in the regular lesson track, not a knowledge gap. Today covers exactly those 4 mechanics: interrupted past actions, quantity phrases that "look plural but act singular," double comparatives, and word order inside embedded questions. These are genuinely new rules, but each one is small and quick to lock in.

Before We Start 🔍

Quick check on something you already know well from ISA Grammar:

"He ___ a book when his friend called him." Which fits?

Right — an action already IN PROGRESS, interrupted by a shorter one. Let's formalise this and add 3 more mechanics. Next →

Mechanic 1 — the interrupted past ⏱️

When one past action was already IN PROGRESS and a second, shorter action interrupts it, the longer/background action takes past continuous ("was/were + -ing"), and the shorter, interrupting action takes past simple.

📖 Background (longer)
was reading, was walking, was sleeping — the action already happening
⚡ Interrupter (shorter)
called, knocked, rang — the sudden action that cuts in
🔑 Bonus mechanic — the "in-progress" passive

A similar idea applies to things being built/done RIGHT NOW: "A hospital is being built in our neighborhood now" — not "is built" (that would mean it's already finished) and not "builds" (that\'s a general habit, not a current one-time project).

"A hospital ___ in our neighborhood now, and traffic congestion ___ around the area because of the ongoing work."

Mechanic 2 — a whole AMOUNT is one thing 💰

A phrase describing a total amount of money, time, or distance — even though it LOOKS plural (has an "-s") — is treated as ONE single amount, so it takes a SINGULAR verb.

🔑 The test

Ask: "am I talking about several separate things, or one total amount?" "Ten dollars" isn't ten separate one-dollar items here — it's one price. "Three hours" isn't three separate hour-blocks — it's one length of time. One amount → singular verb (is/was/has).

"Ten dollars ___ a small price to pay for something ___ you truly happy."
Which underlined part is wrong? "Please remember to display (A) your pass and check the sign to see how long you can stay (B) — usually three hours (C) are allowed (D)."

Mechanic 3 — never stack two comparatives 📏

English only marks "more/-er" ONCE per comparison. Either add "-er" to a short word, or put "more" before a longer word — never both, and never "very more."

✅ Correct
much more enjoyable · far more comfortable · a lot happier · even better
❌ Wrong (double-marked)
very more enjoyable · more happier · more better
Which underlined part is wrong? "The weather this summer is better (A) than last year's, with clearer skies and less humidity (B), making outdoor activities (C) very more enjoyable (D) for everyone."

Mechanic 4 — questions hidden inside a sentence 🔀

When a question gets tucked INSIDE a bigger sentence ("she asked where...", "I wonder if...", "he explained why..."), it stops acting like a question and goes back to normal STATEMENT word order — subject before verb.

❓ Real question
"Where was the theatre?" — verb before subject, question mark
🔀 Embedded (hidden) question
"He asked where the theatre was." — subject before verb, no question mark
Which underlined part is wrong? "A man stopped me (A) in the street and asked (B) if I knew (C) where was the theatre (D)."

Mixed practice — all 4 mechanics together 🎯

1. "She ___ dinner when the phone ___ suddenly."
2. Which underlined part is wrong? "Five kilometres (A) are a long way (B) to walk in this heat, (C) especially without water (D)."
3. Which underlined part is wrong? "The new bridge is (A) much stronger, and traffic flows (B) more smoother now that it's (C) finally open to (D) the public."
4. Which underlined part is wrong? "The teacher (A) wanted to know (B) why were the students (C) so quiet (D) that morning."

Day 3 done! 🎉

4 mechanics: (1) longer action = was/were + -ing, interrupter = simple past. (2) A whole amount (money/time/distance) is ONE thing → singular verb, even if it looks plural. (3) Never stack two comparative markers — no "very more." (4) Inside an embedded question ("asked where..."), word order goes back to statement order: subject before verb.
