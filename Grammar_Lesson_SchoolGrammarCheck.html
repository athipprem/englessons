Why this matters 🎯

Grammar questions are the quickest marks on a paper, because each one has exactly one right answer. You also use the same skills every day: the right word in a message, a sign or a speech makes people understand you the first time. If you know which job a gap needs, you can find the answer without guessing.

WORD FORMS · ROUTE A · PICTURES

Word family trees

One small word is the trunk. Its family grows from it. Every family member does one of three jobs: it names something, it describes something, or it says how.

hope
Tap a branch to see it in a sentence.
excite
Tap a branch to see it in a sentence.
hope → hopeful · hopefully · hopeless
excite → excitement · exciting · excitedly

Same trunk, different job. The gap in the sentence tells you which branch to pick.

WORD FORMS · ROUTE B · THREE STEPS

The trees, as three steps

Route A showed the pictures. Route B is the three moves that pick the branch.

1. Look at the word before the gap.
2. Ask what job the gap needs: a naming word, a describing word, or a how word.
3. Pick the ending: -ment -tion -ness -al -ance = naming · -ful -ous -ing -able -y = describing · -ly = how.

Which way do you go?

The gap comes just before a naming word (a ___ smile)→describing word
The gap is the thing itself (the ___ of the band)→naming word
The gap tells how someone did something (ran ___)→how word (-ly)

Always read the sentence again with your answer in the gap.

THREE QUICK RULES

Pronouns · third forms · -er and -est

doing the action → I, she, he, we, they. receiving it → me, her, him, us, them. owning → my, her, his, our, their.
has / have / had + the third form: ride → rode → ridden · eat → ate → eaten · go → went → gone · write → wrote → written
two things → -er or more. three or more → -est or most. never both: “most funniest” ✗
